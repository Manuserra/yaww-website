Why we go

Miles of beach, and hardly anyone on it

El Cuyo sits inside the Río Lagartos nature reserve, on a strip of sand between the lagoons and the Gulf. Wild palms and dunes run right down to the sea, and you can walk for miles with nothing but the kite boarders gliding by on the breeze.

The village is home to fishermen, flamingos, turtles, dolphins and countless birds. The tourists haven’t heard about it yet, so for the most part you’ll have the beach and the town to yourself. Please keep your lips sealed.

We liked it so much that we opened a house here.

Our edit

Six things we love about El Cuyo

01

First light on the water

The sea is glass until about nine.

In the early morning the sea is flat and still, perfect for a paddleboard or kayak as the first rays come over the horizon.

On retreat Kayaks and SUP boards are at the house all week.

02

Flamingos and pink lagoons

Bring binoculars.

Flamingos feed in their natural habitat in the lagoons of the reserve, a short drive from the pink salt lakes of Las Coloradas.

On retreat We visit the flamingos and Las Coloradas together.

03

Tamales by hand

You eat what you make.

Yucatecan cooking is its own thing: achiote, sour orange, slow-cooked pork, and tamales wrapped in banana leaf.

On retreat A hands-on tamale workshop, then dinner.

04

The village by bike

Sand roads, no traffic, nowhere to be.

The town is best seen by bike: the plaza, the pier, the fishing boats pulled up on the sand, and a coconut on the way home.

On retreat Bicycles are yours for the week.

05

A day in the Mayan lands

Our longest day, and the one guests talk about.

The colonial city of Valladolid, the ruins of Ek Balam and a cenote to cool off after a day of exploring.

On retreat An optional day trip, 9:30am to 9pm.

06

Evenings on the seafront

Go at sunset, when the heat breaks.

When the heat goes out of the day the village comes to the seafront: kids on bikes, families on the benches, and the best tacos come from a stand, not a restaurant.

On retreat A toes-in-the-sand dinner party, with salsa music and a margarita.

Ask the host

Christina’s El Cuyo

She has been bringing guests to the Yucatán since 2008. We asked her for the El Cuyo she would give a friend.

The best hour of the day?
Late afternoon, when the light goes gold and the fishing boats come back in.
The best walk?
Along the shore, away from the village, until the houses stop. After that it is dunes, shells and the odd fisherman.
The best thing to eat?
Breakfast on the terrace, whatever Sonia is cooking that morning.
The best thing to pack?
Pesos and a light sweater. You will live in one pair of sandals.
The best free afternoon?
A rocking chair on the porch and the book you have been saving for it.

Before you come

Good to know, honestly

Rooms
Every room at Utopia shares a bathroom: there are three full bathrooms, a double-sink wash area and an outdoor shower on the beach.
Money
There are no ATMs in El Cuyo and most places prefer cash. Bring Mexican pesos.
Wifi
Some, but don’t count on it. The wifi and the electricity can cut out at times, though they usually come back quickly.
Health
Pack any medication you need. For anything specific, the nearest option is Tizimín, about an hour and a half away.
Time
Cancún is one hour ahead of El Cuyo all year, so check the times when you book your flights.
Pace
Gently active. Yoga every morning and most evenings, and the rest of the day is yours.

El Cuyo suits you if

  • You want miles of beach and hardly anyone on it.
  • A good day is yoga, the sea, a bike ride and a long lunch.
  • You would rather have a fishing village than a resort.

It may not if

  • You are after nightlife, shopping or a big hotel.
  • You need a reliable connection for work.
  • You want your own bathroom.
