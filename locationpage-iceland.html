Why we keep coming back to Iceland

It is the one place on our map where the landscape does the talking. Days are long or barely there, the weather rewrites the plan, and nobody minds. The practice gets quieter here, and so does everyone in it.

We first brought a group in 2019 and have gone back every year since, with the same guesthouse outside Vik and the same guide who grew up on that coast. He is the reason we see the places the buses do not stop at.

It suits travellers who would rather be outside in bad weather than inside in good.

Founder

Beyond the mat in Iceland

Glacier walk

Crampons on, rope between us. Two hours out on the ice with a guide who reads it like a page.

Hot springs

The practice ends and the water starts. Most evenings close in a pool somewhere with the steam coming off it.

Black sand beach practice

Mats on the sand at Reynisfjara, the sea stacks behind you and the wind deciding how long we stay.

Gallery

Reykjavík for the first days, then south to Vik, where the beaches are black and the waterfalls come straight off the road. The photographs are from past retreats: the ice, the horses, and the sky on the nights it decides to perform.

Where you stay

Hotel Kría, Vik

A small guesthouse half an hour from the black beach, run by the family that built it. Twenty rooms, one long table, and a window in the lounge that frames the whole valley. On clear nights nobody goes to bed early.

Rooms and prices are on each retreat.

“He grew up on this coast and still stops the van for the light. Six years in, he is the reason the week feels like a visit rather than a tour.”

Christina Thomas, on Guðmundur · guide, south coast · with YAWW since 2019
